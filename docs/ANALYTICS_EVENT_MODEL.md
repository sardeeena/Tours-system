# Analytics event model

## Two separate analytics domains

1. **Visitor analytics** is consent-gated. It describes public-site browsing and optional attribution. It must not contain customer contact details, booking references, passenger data, or staff-console activity.
2. **Operational lifecycle analytics** records business transactions required to operate and reconcile bookings, payments, and refunds. It is generated by database triggers, stores only a booking UUID plus non-PII event facts, and is not a visitor/session tracker. It is protected by RLS and has no `anon` or `authenticated` table access.

Do not join these domains by booking reference or customer identity. Session-to-booking attribution is a separate privacy/legal design decision and is not part of this migration.

## Operational lifecycle event contract

| Event | Source and trigger | Meaning | Important exclusions |
|---|---|---|---|
| `booking_submitted` | `bookings` INSERT | The reservation was saved by the authoritative booking transaction | Does not mean supplier accepted, booking confirmed, or payment received |
| `booking_status_confirmed` | `bookings.status` transition to `confirmed` | Staff recorded the booking as confirmed in the current supplier-managed workflow | It is not a direct supplier-system callback; no supplier integration currently exists |
| `booking_cancellation_requested` | `bookings.status` transition to `cancellation_requested` | A cancellation request entered the workflow | Not a completed cancellation |
| `booking_cancelled` | `bookings.status` transition to `cancelled` | Cancellation was recorded | Does not imply a refund was processed |
| `booking_completed` | Booking status becomes `completed` while ledger-authoritative payment status is `paid` | The excursion is operationally completed and paid | Never fires for pending/unpaid bookings |
| `booking_no_show` | `bookings.status` transition to `no_show` | Staff recorded a no-show | Not a cancellation |
| `payment_received` | `payment_transactions.status` becomes `Paid` | A settled payment transaction was recorded | Does not imply supplier confirmation or excursion completion |
| `payment_partially_received` | Payment transaction status becomes `Partially Paid` | A partial payment was recorded | Not a full purchase |
| `payment_failed` | Payment transaction status becomes `Failed` | A failed payment transaction was recorded | Does not change booking status by itself |
| `payment_transaction_refunded` | Payment transaction status becomes `Refunded` | A payment transaction was marked refunded | Distinct from the approved refund record being processed |
| `refund_processed` | `refund_records.status` becomes `processed` | Finance recorded a processed refund | A refund request or approval alone is not a processed refund |

The database event key is unique and each source event is written in the same transaction as the business change. Repeated delivery of the same transition is therefore idempotent. The event ledger deliberately omits names, email addresses, phone numbers, booking references, passenger details, and free text.

Legacy `visitor_events.booking_confirmed` records are not authoritative supplier confirmations. Historical inspection found them occurring virtually simultaneously with `booking_submitted`; keep them out of operational conversion and sales reporting. The visitor funnel therefore reports started, completed steps, submitted, and cancellation requests only. Use `booking_status_confirmed` from the operational ledger for the staff-recorded confirmation count.

## Supplier workflow boundary

The current business process is supplier-managed. Staff record the operational booking status after communicating with suppliers. The current schema has no supplier-rejected status and no supplier API callback, so the model does **not** invent a `supplier_booking_rejected` event or claim a direct supplier confirmation. Add those only when a supplier response is represented by an explicit, authenticated workflow field/event.

Vessels and guides remain available for future internal resource management; this event model does not disable or repurpose them.

## GA4 mapping

- `booking_submitted` remains the lead event and maps to the existing `qualify_lead` key event only after a successful authoritative save and visitor analytics consent.
- Do not map pending reservations to `purchase`.
- Do not send supplier/staff operational events to GA4 without an eligible consented session and a privacy-reviewed attribution design.
- `purchase` should be reserved for the point at which the business has actually received the full payment required by the chosen reporting definition. Partial payment, staff confirmation, and excursion completion are separate dimensions.
- Custom dimensions/metrics and key-event administration are GA4 Admin API/UI configuration, not available through the current read-only Windsor.ai reporting connector.

## Reconciliation

Use `booking_lifecycle_events` as the source of truth for operational booking/payment transitions. Use `visitor_events` for consented browsing funnel activity. Use the finance ledger (`payment_transactions` and `refund_records`) for money. Do not reconcile these by comparing raw event counts without aligning date ranges, event semantics, consent coverage, and idempotency.
